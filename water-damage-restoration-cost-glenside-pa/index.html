Water Damage Restoration Cost in Glenside, PA: What Drives the Price

Nobody can quote a fair number from a photo. Here are the five things that decide it and how to read an estimate.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

People search for what water damage restoration costs because they want to know whether an estimate is fair. The truthful answer is that the price depends on facts that cannot be known until someone measures the wet area. This guide explains those facts and does not give a price per square foot, since we have no primary source for one.

We explain the scope and price before work starts, and we encourage you to compare estimates in writing. The goal here is to help you ask better questions.

Five factors that set the price

Water categoryClean water, grey water and sewage need different handling and disposal.
Wet square footageMoisture mapping shows how far the water went.
Materials involvedHardwood, carpet, drywall and plaster dry and fail differently.
Drying days and equipmentMore air movers and dehumidifiers for more days.
Rebuild scopeDrywall, paint, flooring and trim after drying.

Mitigation versus rebuild

An estimate usually has two parts. The first is mitigation, which covers extraction, removal of wet materials and drying. The second is the rebuild, which covers replacing drywall, flooring and finishes.

Questions to ask any company

  • Is the scope in writing before work starts?
  • Will you share the moisture log at the end?
  • Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500?
  • What is not included, such as the plumbing repair?

A company that cannot answer these is not ready to be paid. In Pennsylvania, contractors who do more than $5,000 of work a year must register with the Attorney General, so ask for proof.

Insurance and the deductible

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage usually needs a separate flood policy. Your deductible decides how much of the estimate comes out of pocket, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

Do not assume a claim will be approved, and keep every receipt. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department also advises photographing the damage before cleanup.

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Stop the water fast, since less water means a smaller job
  • Start drying within 24 to 48 hours, which the EPA advises
  • Keep wet materials that can be saved, and ask for readings
  • Document the loss for the claim

Starting early often keeps materials that would otherwise need replacement. If you want to see how drying runs, read our structural drying page, and for the first step see water extraction.

Two scenarios, described without prices

A supply line under a second-floor bathroom sink fails and is shut off within the hour. Clean water, one cabinet and a short stretch of flooring are wet. The job is mostly extraction, a few days of drying and a small repair.

A roof leak runs through a weekend and soaks the attic insulation and two bedroom ceilings. The job adds insulation removal, ceiling repairs in two rooms and a longer drying period. The first scenario is small, and the second has more of every factor on the list.

Red flags in an estimate

  • No written scope, only a total
  • No mention of drying equipment or the number of days
  • A promise that the insurer will approve it
  • Pressure to sign immediately, or a "free storm inspection" pitch

What to do before you ask for estimates

  • Stop the source and photograph the damage.
  • Write down which rooms and floors are wet.
  • Ask each company for the scope in writing.
  • Check your policy for the deductible.

Why scope is more important than the total

Two estimates with the same total can cover different work. One might include removal of wet insulation and a week of drying, while the other leaves them out and bills them later as an extra. Reading the lines is the only way to compare.

Ask each company to write the scope as a list: what will be removed, what equipment will run, for how many days and what is left for repair. Then ask what could change the number, such as finding more wet material when a wall is opened.

Costs in the Glenside area

Costs depend on the house and the loss, not on the address. Abington and Cheltenham townships each handle permits, so ask the building department whether your rebuild needs one.

If your home is in Ambler or Oreland, the same factors apply. Check the FEMA flood map for your address if flooding is a concern.
