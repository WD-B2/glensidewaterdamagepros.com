How to Document Water Damage for an Insurance Claim in Glenside, PA

A claim is only as strong as the record behind it. This checklist covers what to capture before anyone starts cleaning.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

(445) 263-2078

A water damage claim goes better when the record is complete. Adjusters look at photos, receipts, moisture logs and the timeline of what happened. Most of that evidence disappears the moment cleanup starts, so the best time to document is before you touch anything.

This guide follows advice from the Pennsylvania Insurance Department and adds practical steps that homeowners in Glenside and nearby towns can use. We are a restoration company, not an insurer, and we cannot promise any claim outcome. Your policy and your insurer decide coverage.

Before cleanup: photograph and record

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing or recording video of the damage before cleanup. Take wide shots of each room, then close-ups of stains, wet materials and the source of the water. Include something for scale, such as a tape measure, and narrate what you see in the video.

  • The source: the failed pipe, hose, roof area or drain
  • Every wet room, from the doorway and from the corners
  • Damaged belongings, with brand labels visible where you can
  • Standing water depth, with a ruler in the shot
  • Meters, if you have them, such as a water meter reading

Contact your insurer promptly

The department advises contacting your insurer promptly after damage. Ask what the policy requires, how to file and whether the insurer wants to inspect before repairs. Write down the claim number, the date and the name of the person you spoke with.

Do not assume a claim will be approved, and do not sign anything you do not understand. If a contractor says the insurer has approved their work, ask the insurer yourself.

Temporary repairs only, and save every receipt

The department advises making temporary repairs only until the insurer has seen the damage. A tarp over a roof opening or a plug on a leaking pipe protects the house without destroying evidence. Keep every receipt for materials, equipment and temporary help.

Make a contents list

List damaged belongings with a description, the age and an estimate of what it cost. Photograph serial numbers on electronics. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe, such as items touched by sewage.

Items touched by floodwater or sewage are usually discarded, and the CDC gives the same direction for materials contaminated by floodwater. Photograph them before disposal and write down what they were.

Flood versus water damage coverage

Flood damage is not covered by most homeowners policies, and the department notes that separate flood coverage exists. Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a pipe that burst this week, is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Check your policy, and ask your insurer to explain anything unclear.

Choosing a contractor carefully

The department warns about "free storm inspection" scams after weather events. Contractors who do more than $5,000 of work a year in Pennsylvania must register with the Attorney General, so ask for proof. Ask for scope and price in writing before work starts.

A thirty-minute documentation routine

  1. Walk each room with your phone and record video while narrating the damage.
  2. Take still photos of the source, the stains, wet materials and damaged items.
  3. Write down the date, the time you found the damage and what you did first.
  4. Start a folder for receipts, the claim number and every person you speak with.
  5. Ask the crew for the moisture log and the scope of work in writing.

Mistakes that weaken a record

  • Throwing out damaged items before photographing them
  • Discarding the failed part, such as a hose, before the adjuster asks
  • Starting permanent repairs before the insurer has seen the loss
  • Relying on verbal promises from anyone, including a contractor

A restoration company can supply moisture readings, photos of what was removed and an itemized scope. Those documents support your claim, though the insurer decides what is covered.

Advice here follows the Pennsylvania Insurance Department guidelines for documenting a loss, and the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the reference for the drying records a crew should keep.
