Water under the sink or behind the cabinets

A kitchen is full of water lines, and a leak in one can run for days behind cabinets before anyone sees it. Kitchen water damage restoration dries the cabinets, subfloor and flooring and repairs what could not be saved. If water is running now, close the shutoff valve under the sink or the main and call.

A burst pipe under a sink releases a lot of water quickly, and the toe-kick at the base of the cabinets can hide the spread. Pull the items from under the sink, take photos and tell the dispatcher what flooring runs through the kitchen.

Swollen cabinet bases

Particleboard cabinet bases swell and lose their finish when wet.

Cupping flooring near the sink

Boards bow upward as they take on water.

Musty smell under the sink

Odor suggests a slow leak.

Soft or stained subfloor

Pressing near the dishwasher shows whether the wood is soft.

What gets wet and what can be saved

Solid wood cabinets and plywood often dry and stay. Particleboard cabinets with a laminate finish swell and rarely recover. Granite and quartz tops stay, but the cabinets under them may need removal to dry.

Moisture meters and a thermal camera show water behind the toe-kicks and under the flooring. Crews remove the toe-kicks, drill small holes in the cabinet bases and place air movers and a dehumidifier. The leak source itself is covered on our appliance leak page when a dishwasher or fridge line failed.

Under-sink leaks, step by step

A sink supply line, a drain trap or a disposal can all leak, and a slow drip often stays unseen behind the cabinet face. Empty the cabinet, take photos, then press the floor of the cabinet with a finger. Swollen or soft particleboard is a sign the leak has run for a while.

Crews remove the toe-kick, check the wall behind the cabinet and look at the subfloor from beneath if there is a basement below. A plumber fixes the line, and drying starts once the water has stopped.

Dishwasher and refrigerator spaces

Water from a dishwasher often travels under the adjacent cabinets. Water from a refrigerator ice maker line often shows up as cupped flooring several feet from the fridge. Pulling the appliance out and checking the floor and the wall behind it is part of the first visit.

Countertops, backsplash and finishes

Stone and solid-surface tops are rarely damaged by water. The cabinet base under them can be, and replacing a base without lifting the top is often possible. Tile backsplash can hide moisture behind it, and a meter reading through the grout lines tells you if the wall behind is wet.

What to do before we arrive

  • Close the shutoff valve under the sink or the main.
  • Unplug small appliances on wet counters.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or disposal.
  • Empty the cabinet and photograph the area.

Drying under cabinets follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and it is reasonable to ask for the moisture readings that show the cabinet base reached dry.
