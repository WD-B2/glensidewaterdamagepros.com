Stained, soft or bubbling walls

Drywall takes up water through its edges and from behind, and a wall can look fine on the outside while the cavity is wet. Wall and drywall water damage repair dries the cavity, cuts out drywall that cannot recover and rebuilds. If the wall is wet from a leak that is still running, close the shutoff valve or the main and call.

Press the wall near the baseboard with your finger. Soft, crumbly or bowed drywall is past drying, while firm drywall that is only stained can often dry in place. A moisture meter gives the real answer.

Paint bubbling or peeling

Water pushing out from behind lifts the paint.

Soft spot near the baseboard

The drywall has lost its strength.

Brown or yellow stains

Staining shows water has traveled through the board.

A damp, musty smell

Odor suggests wet insulation or framing.

How a wet wall is dried

Crews map moisture with a thermal camera and meters. They remove the baseboard, drill small holes at the bottom of the cavity and push air through with air movers while a dehumidifier handles the room. If insulation is wet, they cut the drywall and remove it.

Drywall can often be cut about a foot above the visible water line and replaced in sections. Plaster in older houses is dried more slowly, and the wall may be left open until readings are dry. For a wall wet from a plumbing failure, see our burst pipe page, and for ceilings above, see ceiling repair.

What happens behind the baseboard

Water that reaches a wall usually collects at the bottom plate and wicks up through the drywall like a candle wick. The baseboard hides the line where this starts, so crews remove it first. A flashlight and a meter at the base of each stud bay show how far the water climbed.

Wiring and plumbing sit in the same cavities. If the water reached outlets or switches in the wall, the electrician should check them before the wall is closed. We do not do electrical work, and we say when a trade is needed.

From patch to paint

  1. Cut the damaged drywall back to dry, solid material and to the nearest stud.
  2. Dry the cavity until readings match a dry reference wall.
  3. Install new drywall and tape and finish the joints.
  4. Prime with a stain-blocking primer so old stains do not bleed through.
  5. Paint the full wall corner to corner so the patch does not show.

Matching texture is the part that takes skill. A flat wall hides a patch easily, and a heavy orange peel or knockdown texture needs practice. Ask to see the plan for the finish before you approve the work.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration guides when drywall can be dried in place and when it must be cut out, and we log readings so you can see that decision.
