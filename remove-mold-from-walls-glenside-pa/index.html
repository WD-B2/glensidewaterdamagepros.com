How to Remove Mold From Walls in Glenside, PA: What the EPA Advises

Mold on a wall is a moisture problem. Here is what the EPA says about when to clean it yourself and when to stop.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

Mold on a wall usually follows water. A leak behind the wall, condensation on a cold exterior wall or a basement wall that stays damp can all supply it. The EPA says the key to mold control is moisture control, so removing the mold without fixing the water is a short-lived fix.

This guide follows the EPA's published guidance and adds practical notes for homeowners in Glenside, PA. We do not give medical advice, and health questions belong with your physician.

Step one: find and fix the water

Before cleaning anything, find the source. Look for a leaking pipe, a roof stain, a window that lets in rain or an air conditioner drain that backs up. If the wall is still wet, drying it comes first, and the EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours.

A moisture meter tells you whether the wall is still wet. If you do not have one, a crew can check, and our structural drying page explains how a wall is dried.

How big is it? The 10 square foot guide

The EPA suggests considering a professional when the mold covers more than 10 square feet, which is about a three-by-three foot patch. It also suggests a professional when mold may be inside walls or when contamination is from sewage. Smaller patches on hard surfaces are the ones homeowners sometimes handle.

What the EPA says about the cleaning itself

  • Dry the area completely after cleaning
  • Do not paint or caulk over mold
  • Porous materials such as carpet and ceiling tiles may need to be discarded
  • If visible mold is present, sampling is usually unnecessary

Painting over mold hides it and traps moisture. Caulk does the same. If growth returns after painting, the wall is still getting moisture from somewhere.

Why drywall is often cut out

Drywall is porous, so mold can grow into the paper. Surface cleaning rarely removes it all, and drywall that stayed wet is often cut out and replaced. A professional uses containment and negative air machines with HEPA filtration to keep spores from reaching other rooms.

If the wall is large or the growth keeps coming back, see our mold remediation page for how the work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard. If the cause was a wet wall from a leak, see wall and drywall water damage repair.

After cleanup: keep the wall dry

  • Fix plumbing leaks and roof leaks
  • Use exhaust fans in bathrooms and kitchens
  • Keep furniture a few inches off cold exterior walls
  • Ask your insurer whether the cause was covered

What a small cleanup looks like on a hard surface

For a small patch on a hard, nonporous surface, the EPA describes scrubbing with detergent and water and then drying the area completely. The aim is to remove the growth and leave no moisture behind. The cleaning does not matter if the source of water is still active.

What not to do

  • Do not paint or caulk over mold
  • Do not cut into a wall before you know what pipes or wiring are behind it
  • Do not ignore the source of moisture
  • Do not disturb a large patch, since that can spread spores through the house

If you are unsure whether the wall hides pipes, a crew can open a small inspection hole and look with a camera. That is cheaper than finding out the hard way.

When walls keep getting damp

A wall that keeps getting damp has a cause. Common ones are condensation on a cold exterior wall, a slow plumbing leak or water entering through a window. Find the cause before cleaning again.

Moving furniture a few inches from an exterior wall, running a bathroom fan and fixing a leaking window are simple steps that cut moisture. If none of those solve it, a crew can map the wall with a thermal camera.

Mold and moisture in Glenside

Glenside is in Montgomery County, where humid summers help moisture linger in basements. Abington Township's stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood as a flooded area, so basements there are worth checking after heavy rain.

If you live in Wyncote or Ambler, where creek and storm moisture affect basements, check lower walls first. Ask your state whether it licenses mold work before hiring.
