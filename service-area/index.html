Townships and borough, and why it matters on a loss

Permits, sewer service and water supply depend on the municipality, and our service area crosses several. Glenside is split between Abington and Cheltenham townships. Wyncote is in Cheltenham Township, Flourtown is in Springfield Township, Oreland sits in both Springfield and Upper Dublin townships, Dresher is in Upper Dublin Township and Ambler is its own borough.

Aqua supplies water in Abington and Cheltenham, and most of Springfield Township. Upper Dublin is served by Aqua Pennsylvania, Ambler Borough Water Company and North Wales Water Authority, and Ambler Borough runs its own water and sewer departments. PECO supplies electricity and gas in the area.

Permit rules vary, so ask the building department of your own township before a repair. Abington Code Enforcement handles building permits in Abington, and Springfield Township Building and Zoning is at 1510 Paper Mill Road in Wyndmoor.

What is the same everywhere

  • Shut off the water, and call the 24/7 line.
  • Stay out of water near outlets and call PECO or 911 for electrical risk.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup and save receipts.
  • Ask for the scope and price before work starts.

Picking the right town page

Use the page for the town you live in, since each one explains its own drainage, utilities and housing. Wyncote leans on older construction, Oreland on the two-township split and Flourtown on Springfield Township's sewer rules. Dresher covers storm water at the Sandy Run headwaters, and Ambler covers creek-area and commercial losses.

If you live in Glenside itself, start with the homepage and the services page. They explain how a loss runs from the first call to the last repair.

How coverage works

  • Call the 24/7 line and give your street address.
  • The dispatcher confirms coverage and asks what happened.
  • Scope and price are explained before work starts.
  • Crews are sent when available, and you will hear what to do while you wait.

Coverage decisions follow the address, the township and the road access, and not only the ZIP code. A single ZIP such as 19038 can cross a municipal line, and the township building department, not our dispatcher, decides which permit rules apply to plumbing or structural work.

Water damage work is guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration in every town we serve. Whatever the address, you can ask us how the crew will dry, what readings will be logged and when a wet material must be removed, and we will explain it before work begins.

If you are between towns, call anyway. Boundaries between townships and ZIP codes do not follow the roads, so the dispatcher decides by address. Our services page lists everything we do.
