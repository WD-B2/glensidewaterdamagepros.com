Frozen pipe emergencies and the thaw problem

Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure can crack a pipe along its length or push a fitting apart. The crack is often hidden inside an exterior wall, an attic or an unheated crawl space. A pipe can sit split for hours with no visible leak until the room warms up.

Faucet flow reduced to a trickle

A partially frozen line gives a weak flow, and the split may already exist behind it.

Frost on an exposed pipe

Visible frost means the pipe is cold enough to freeze solid. Open the cabinet doors and let room heat reach it.

Bulging or cracked pipe

A bulge or hairline split will leak when the ice melts. Close the main before thawing.

Damp spot on a ceiling or wall

A stain at the base of an exterior wall after a cold snap suggests a thaw leak.

Do not thaw with an open flame

Never use a torch or open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low, a heating pad or warm air from a space heater placed safely away from water are the usual choices, and a plumber should handle anything you cannot reach. If the pipe has already split, shut off the main before applying any heat.

How the cleanup works

Crews extract standing water, then map the wet areas with a thermal camera and moisture meters. Exterior walls and cold-space ceilings need extra attention, because insulation soaks up water and holds it against the framing. Wet insulation is removed and replaced, since it does not dry well in place.

Drying equipment then runs for several days, usually air movers to push air across surfaces and dehumidifiers to pull the moisture out of the room. Readings are taken daily, and the equipment comes out only when the materials return to dry standards. The structural drying page explains the equipment in plain words.

Preventing a second freeze

After a freeze, protect the same pipe before the next cold night. Insulate exposed lines, seal drafts where pipes enter the house and let faucets drip on the coldest nights. A plumber can reroute a line that sits in a bad spot, and the work often needs a permit, so ask the township building department.

What to do before the thaw

  • Close the main valve before you apply any heat to a frozen pipe.
  • Open the faucets so melting water has somewhere to go.
  • Do not use a torch or open flame.
  • Keep a bucket and towels at the lowest point of the pipe.
