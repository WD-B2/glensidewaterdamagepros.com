When floodwater is an emergency

Water that entered from outside is treated as contaminated. Surface runoff carries soil, fuel and bacteria, and the CDC advises discarding drywall, rugs and cloth that floodwater touched. Wear boots and gloves, and do not let children or pets wade through it.

What flood restoration involves

Crews pump out the water, then remove the materials that cannot be cleaned. That includes carpet, pad, insulation and drywall below the waterline. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated, and the structure is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Moisture meters and thermal imaging find water that is still inside walls, and readings are logged until materials reach dry standards. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Flooding and local drainage

Abington Township's stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood and the Huntingdon Valley Shopping Center drainage area as places that have experienced flooding. The township also used $7 million in FEMA funds and $1 million of its own to buy homes in the floodplain in 2003. Check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov, since a street away can be mapped differently.

The Wissahickon Creek runs through Ambler and nearby towns, which is why our page for Ambler pays attention to creek-adjacent homes. We do not publish flood zone or creek level figures here, because they change with each map update and storm.

Safety checklist for floodwater

  • Wear boots and gloves, as the CDC advises, if you must enter.
  • Do not touch food or items that floodwater reached.
  • Keep children and pets away from the water.
  • Call PECO or 911 before touching anything electrical.
