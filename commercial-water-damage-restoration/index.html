A water loss in a business is an emergency because every hour of downtime costs money and tenants, staff and stock are at risk. Commercial water damage restoration extracts water, protects contents and dries the structure so the space can reopen, and our 24/7 line answers day and night. Shut off the water at the main, turn off power to wet areas if it is safe and move inventory off the floor.

When a business water loss is an emergency

Water through a shared ceiling

In a multi-unit building, a leak upstairs becomes several claims quickly.

Standing water near equipment

Electronics and machines need power off and a safe approach.

Sprinkler or supply line failure

High-volume leaks release water until the valve is closed.

Backed-up drains in a restaurant or shop

Contaminated water needs category 3 handling.

How a commercial job differs from a house

The drying principles are the same, but the priorities shift. Crews work around business hours, protect records and inventory, and keep access paths open. Floors are often tile, vinyl or commercial carpet, and ceilings may be suspended tile with insulation above.

Large open spaces need more equipment, and multi-tenant buildings need coordination with property management. We document the loss with moisture logs and photos, which supports the building owner's insurance claim and any claim between tenants.

Large loss and insurance assessment

A large loss means many rooms or floors are wet, and several parties are involved. Start by photographing the damage, calling your insurer and keeping a record of every expense. Insurers often send an adjuster, so keep the moisture log and photos ready.

We explain the scope and price before work starts, and we document what we find. The decision on what the insurer pays belongs to the insurer, and we do not promise approval. For a storm-driven loss through a roof or windows, see storm water damage cleanup, and for the drying process itself, see structural drying.

Document the loss in a way that helps the building owner, tenants and insurers. Photograph each unit, label the photos by room and keep a log of what was moved. A single shared record saves arguments later.

What to do before we arrive at your business

  • Tell staff and tenants which rooms to avoid.
  • Shut off the main or the sprinkler branch if you know how.
  • Move inventory, records and electronics off the floor.
  • Call the building owner and your insurer, and keep the numbers handy.

Commercial drying follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as residential work, and property managers can ask any contractor for the written drying plan that applies it.
