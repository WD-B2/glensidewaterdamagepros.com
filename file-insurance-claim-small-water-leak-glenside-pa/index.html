Should You File an Insurance Claim for a Small Water Leak in Glenside, PA?

A small leak is not always small. Here is how to decide whether to file, and what to do either way.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

A drip under a sink or a stain on a ceiling does not feel like an insurance event. Many homeowners wonder whether the repair will cost less than the deductible and whether a claim is worth the trouble. The answer depends on facts you may not have yet.

We are not insurance advisers. This guide helps you think through the question and suggests who to ask. Your policy and insurer decide coverage.

Why small leaks can be bigger than they look

A leak behind a wall can soak insulation and framing for days before a stain shows. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours, because waiting lets mold start. A repair that looks like a patch may involve cutting out drywall and drying a cavity.

Moisture meters and a thermal camera can show how far the water went. That reading is the difference between a small repair and a larger one.

Document either way

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing or recording the damage before cleanup, saving receipts and contacting your insurer promptly. Even if you decide not to file, a record protects you if the problem grows. Keep the plumber's invoice and the moisture log.

Ask before you decide

  • Ask your insurer whether the loss would be covered if it were reported
  • Ask how reporting a loss may affect your record
  • Ask whether there is a deadline for reporting
  • Ask for the answers in writing if you can

Asking questions does not always mean filing a claim, but ask how the insurer treats an inquiry.

Fix the source first

Whatever you decide on the claim, stop the leak. A plumber repairs a failed pipe, and a roofer repairs a roof. For the damage, see our wall and drywall repair page or kitchen water damage page, depending on where it happened.

A short decision worksheet

  1. Write down the likely cost of repair from at least one written scope.
  2. Find your deductible on the policy declarations page.
  3. Note whether the cause looks sudden or gradual.
  4. Ask your insurer what a report would mean for coverage and your record.
  5. Decide, and keep the paperwork either way.

If you decide not to file

Keep the photos, the plumber's invoice and any moisture reading. If the damage grows later, those records show when it started and what you did. Tell your insurer promptly if a small loss turns out to be larger.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting weeks to look at a stain
  • Repairing without photographing first
  • Assuming a contractor can decide coverage
  • Forgetting that a hidden leak may be bigger than the stain

The guidelines from the Pennsylvania Insurance Department on contacting your insurer promptly and keeping receipts apply to small losses too, and the EPA drying window is the reason a small leak should not wait.

Real-world example: a slow leak behind a washer

A homeowner notices a faint stain on the basement ceiling below the laundry room. The repair looks like a patch and a coat of paint, which costs less than the deductible. A moisture reading shows the subfloor and two joist bays are wet, and the scope grows to include removal of a section of ceiling and several days of drying.

The lesson is not that every leak should become a claim. It is that you do not know the size of a loss until it has been measured, so measure first and decide second.

What to say when you call your insurer

Describe what happened in plain facts, with the date you found it and what you did to stop it. Ask what the policy covers, whether a report would count as a claim and what the next steps are. Write down what you were told and the name of the person who told you.
