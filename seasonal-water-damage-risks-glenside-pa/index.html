Seasonal Water Damage Risks for Glenside, PA Homes

Each season puts a different kind of water in the house. Here is what to watch and when.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

Water damage does not follow one calendar. In the Glenside area, winter brings frozen pipes and ice, spring brings rain on saturated ground, summer brings storms and air conditioner condensate and fall brings clogged gutters. Knowing the season helps you check the right place before a small problem becomes a claim.

This guide is general and does not claim local frequency figures. For precise local weather records, the National Weather Service and NOAA publish the normals for the area.

Winter: freezing pipes and ice dams

Pipes in exterior walls, attics and unheated garages freeze first. Water expands as it freezes, and the split often reveals itself only after the thaw. Ice dams on roof edges push meltwater under shingles and into ceilings.

  • Insulate exposed pipes and seal drafts
  • Open cabinet doors on cold nights
  • Keep heat on if you travel
  • Know where the main shutoff is

If a pipe has already split, our frozen pipe damage page covers the steps.

Spring: rain, seepage and sump pumps

Wet ground sends water toward foundations. Basements that stay dry in summer can seep after weeks of spring rain, and a sump pump that has not run since last year may fail when it matters. Test the pump by pouring water in the pit and check that the discharge line is clear.

Summer: storms and condensate

Summer thunderstorms can drive rain through roofs, windows and window wells. Air conditioners produce condensate, and a clogged drain line sends it into a ceiling or closet. A yearly flush of the drain line before cooling season is a simple habit.

The EF-2 tornado that hit the Ambler area in 2021 came during the aftermath of Hurricane Ida, a reminder that severe weather can reach the area in late summer. See our storm water damage page for what to do if rain gets in.

Fall: gutters, leaves and roof checks

Leaves clog gutters and downspouts, and overflowing water runs down walls and into basements. A fall check of gutters, flashings and vents prevents winter leaks. Check the attic for stains after the first heavy rain.

A yearly water checklist

  • Winter: locate the main shutoff, insulate pipes in cold spaces and keep heat on when you travel
  • Spring: test the sump pump, check the basement walls after the first heavy rain and clear window wells
  • Summer: flush the AC condensate line, inspect the roof after storms and check under sinks
  • Fall: clean gutters, point downspouts away from the foundation and check attic stains

What to do at the first sign

A stain, a smell or a drip is a prompt to look, not a mystery to wait out. Find the source, stop the water and photograph the damage. The sooner a wet material is dried, the less of it needs replacing, and the EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours.

For weather records, the guidelines and normals published by NOAA and the National Weather Service are the right reference, and the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the document for how drying should follow a loss.

Why the shoulder seasons are easy to miss

Fall and spring feel quiet, but they carry hidden risks. A gutter clogged with leaves in October overflows on the first heavy rain in November. A sump pump that sat idle all winter may fail in April, when the ground thaws and water moves.

A fifteen-minute walk around the house twice a year, once in fall and once in spring, catches most of these. Look at gutters, downspouts, window wells, the sump pit and the basement walls.

Signs the house is telling you something

  • A musty smell in a closet that did not have one last year
  • Peeling paint on an exterior wall
  • Water beads on a basement window after rain
  • A sudden jump in your water bill

Seasonal notes for Glenside

Abington Township's stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood as a place that has flooded, and the township bought homes in the floodplain with $7 million of FEMA funds and $1 million of its own in 2003. If your house is near the Keswick area, spring and summer rain deserve extra attention.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address at msc.fema.gov. Nearby Dresher sits at the headwaters of Sandy Run, and Ambler lies along the Wissahickon Creek, so see those pages if you live there.
