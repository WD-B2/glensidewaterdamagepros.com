Standing water is an emergency because it soaks into subfloors, drywall and furniture legs within hours. Water extraction is the step that pumps or vacuums the liquid out of the building before drying can begin, and our 24/7 line sends crews day and night. While you wait, shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source, lift valuables off wet floors and keep people and pets out of the water.

This page covers pulling out standing water. If you already know the source, the pages for a burst pipe or a failed water heater add the shutoff steps that belong to each one.

When standing water is an emergency

Water above the baseboards

Water that reaches the baseboards is already moving into the wall cavity behind them. Extraction should start the same day.

Water near outlets or the panel

Electricity and standing water do not mix, and the room should be treated as unsafe until power is off.

A musty smell after a day

Odor means the materials have stayed wet long enough for microbes to start. The EPA advises drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours.

Water coming from a drain

Water that rises from a floor drain or toilet may carry sewage, which changes the plan. See sewage backup below.

Power and water: stay out first

If water reaches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, do not wade in. Turn power off at the main only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry floor. Call PECO or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line.

How water extraction works in a house

The method follows the depth. A few inches of water on a hard floor comes out with a wet vacuum and a squeegee wand, and deeper water goes to a submersible pump with a discharge hose to the outside. Large volumes in a basement or a commercial space call for a truck-mounted extraction unit that moves far more water per minute than anything portable.

Carpet and pad hold water like a sponge, so crews use a weighted extraction wand that presses water out of the fibers. Once the liquid is gone, moisture meters and a thermal camera show what is still wet inside walls and under floors, because a surface that looks dry can hold far more water behind it.

Extraction alone is never the finish line. After the standing water is gone, structural drying and dehumidification removes the moisture that soaked into framing and subfloors, and that stage decides whether mold gets a chance.

Water categories change the plan

Water is sorted into three categories. Category 1 comes from a clean supply line, category 2 carries some contamination, such as washing machine discharge, and category 3 includes sewage and floodwater. Category 3 water means porous materials like carpet and drywall are usually discarded, and the CDC gives the same direction for materials touched by floodwater.

If the water came from a toilet or a floor drain, read our page on sewage backup cleanup before touching anything. For rising water from rain, flood damage cleanup covers what is different.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration describes extraction as the first stage of a loss, and it is the document to ask a company about when you compare estimates.
