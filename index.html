The first 72 hours

Water damage restoration in Glenside, step by step

Most of the damage in a wet house happens in the first two to three days, so the order of work matters more than any single tool. This is how a typical water damage repair in Glenside runs from the first call to the last repair.

1

Call and stop the source

The dispatcher asks what happened, how much is wet and where the shutoff is. You stop the water if it is safe to do so.

2

Extract and assess

Crews pump out standing water, then map moisture with meters and thermal cameras to find what is hidden.

3

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for days while readings are logged until the wood and drywall reach dry targets.

4

Repair and rebuild

Wet drywall, pad and flooring that cannot be saved are replaced, and the rooms are put back together.

Cost and insurance

What shapes the price of water damage repair

Nobody can quote a fair number for a wet house from a phone description alone. Five factors decide most of it.

Water categoryClean supply water, grey water and sewage are handled differently, and the category sets how much material must be removed.
Square footage wetMoisture mapping shows how far the water traveled, and it is often farther than the visible stain.
Materials involvedHardwood, carpet, drywall and plaster dry differently, and some cannot be saved.
Drying days and equipmentMore air movers and dehumidifiers running for more days raise the drying portion of the job.
Rebuild scopeDrywall, paint, flooring and trim after drying are the repair side of the bill.

Local knowledge

Glenside homes and why water finds them

Glenside is a Census-designated place with 7,737 residents in the 2020 Census, and it sits across two townships, Abington and Cheltenham, in Montgomery County. Which township your house is in decides who handles permits and sewer questions. When a repair needs a permit, ask the township building department before work starts.

The Keswick Theatre opened in 1928 and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1983, a reminder of how long Glenside has been built up. Houses from that era often have plaster walls and original drain lines, and plaster dries more slowly than modern drywall. That is why we log moisture readings in plaster rooms before anyone talks about closing a wall.

Abington Township's own stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood and the Huntingdon Valley Shopping Center drainage area as places that have flooded. If you live near either one, take basement seepage seriously after a heavy rain and look at our basement cleanup page. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, because a nearby street can be mapped very differently from yours.

Aqua supplies water in Abington and Cheltenham, and PECO supplies electricity and gas. Knowing both matters during a loss: the main water shutoff stops a supply-line leak, and PECO is the call for any electrical or gas risk before you wade into a wet room.
