Buying a Home in Glenside, PA: How to Spot Hidden Water Damage

A fresh coat of paint can hide a lot. Here is where buyers can look for signs of past water.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

A house can look perfect at a showing and still hide water damage behind a new coat of paint. Buyers who know where to look can ask better questions and decide when to bring in an inspector. This checklist covers the signs that matter and the questions to put to the seller.

We do not inspect homes for sale, and this guide does not replace a licensed home inspection. It helps you decide what to ask and what to look at.

Signs to look for on a walk-through

Stains and rings

Brown or yellow rings on a ceiling or wall mean water reached it.

Musty odor

A closed-up smell in a basement or closet suggests lingering moisture.

Paint that does not match

Fresh paint on one patch can cover a stain.

Soft or warped flooring

Cupped boards or soft spots near walls point to past leaks.

Check the basement and lower walls

Look at the base of basement walls for white mineral crusting, tide lines and rust on metal near the floor. Check whether the floor slopes toward a drain and look at the sump pump. A sump pit with a fresh pump and a clear discharge line is a good sign, while a dry pit that smells is a question.

If you see crusting or stains, ask whether the seller has had water in the basement before. The answer helps you decide whether to bring in a waterproofing specialist.

Look up, under and behind

  • Ceilings under bathrooms and the roof
  • Under sinks and around the dishwasher
  • Around windows and exterior doors
  • The attic, for stains on roof decking

Use a flashlight in the cabinet under the sink and press gently on the base. Soft particleboard suggests a slow leak.

A seller who can show receipts and a moisture log gives you a much better picture. If the answers are vague, ask your inspector to check the area with a moisture meter and thermal camera.

If you find damage

Ask for the cost to be priced before closing. The issue may be small, such as a stain from a fixed leak, or larger, such as hidden mold. See our mold remediation page if growth is present and basement water damage if the lower level is the concern.

Questions for your inspector

  • Can you scan the ceilings and lower walls with a thermal camera and moisture meter?
  • Do you see signs of past repair, such as patched drywall or fresh paint on one wall?
  • What condition are the sump pump, the water heater and the supply lines in?
  • Do the grading and downspouts send water away from the house?

Reading a seller disclosure alongside the walk-through

A disclosure form tells you what the seller says they know. The walk-through tells you what you can see. Compare them, and ask about any mismatch. If a form says no water problems and the basement has a tide line, ask for an explanation in writing.

After closing

If you find damage after closing, document it, call your insurer if it looks like a covered loss and get a written scope. A dry, stable stain from an old event can be cosmetic, while an active leak needs prompt action.

For reference, the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the document restoration crews use, and a seller who had a past leak dried can usually say whether the work followed it.

Sump pumps, grading and the outside of the house

Walk the outside before you leave. Look at where downspouts end, whether the ground slopes away from the foundation and whether window wells are clear. Water that runs toward a house is the usual cause of a wet basement.

Ask about the sump pump, if the house has one. How old is it, does it have a battery backup and when was it last tested? A pump that cannot be tested is a question to put to the seller.

Buying in the Glenside area

Glenside is a census-designated place split between Abington and Cheltenham townships, so ask which township your house is in. Abington Code Enforcement handles building permits on its side, and permit records can show past repairs.

Check the FEMA flood map for the property address. Abington's stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood as flooded, so ask about drainage there. Nearby Flourtown and Wyncote have their own township rules, which our pages explain.
