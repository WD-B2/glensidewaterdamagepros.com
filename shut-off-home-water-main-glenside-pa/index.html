How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Glenside, PA

The fastest way to limit water damage is to stop the water. Here is how to find and use your main valve.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

When a pipe fails, every minute of flow adds to the damage. The most useful thing a homeowner can do is stop the water at the main, and that only works if you already know where it is. Take ten minutes now to find it and test it.

This guide covers where main valves are usually found, the two valve types and how to confirm the water is off. It also covers what not to touch.

Where to look

The main shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house. In a house with a basement, look on the wall facing the street, near the water meter. In a house on a slab or crawl space, it may be in a utility closet, a garage or near the water heater.

  • Basement front wall near the meter
  • Utility room or garage
  • Inside a crawl space access
  • An outdoor meter box, which the water company often controls

Label it with a tag so everyone in the house can find it.

Two kinds of valves

Valve types
ValveHow it looksHow to close it
Gate valveRound wheel handleTurn clockwise several times until it stops
Ball valveLever handleTurn a quarter turn so the lever is across the pipe

Gate valves can stick or break if they have not moved in years, so exercise yours once a year. Turn it gently and do not force it. If it will not move, call a plumber and keep a bucket and towels ready.

Confirm the water is off

  1. Open a faucet on the lowest floor and let the pipes drain.
  2. Open a faucet on the top floor to let air in.
  3. Check the water meter. If the dial still moves with everything closed, water is leaking.
  4. Turn off the water heater power or gas if the tank is draining.

Do not touch the utility's valve

The valve at the curb or in the meter pit belongs to the water company. Ask the company before touching it. If you cannot close the main in the house, call the company and a plumber.

After the water stops

Photograph the damage, lift belongings and call for help. If water is standing, see our water extraction page. If a pipe in the wall failed, see burst pipe water damage. Keep people away from water near outlets and call PECO or 911 for electrical risk.

A ten-minute drill

  1. Walk to the main valve and look at the handle.
  2. Close it gently, then open a faucet to confirm the flow stops.
  3. Open the valve fully again, and check for drips at the packing.
  4. Label the valve and take a photo for everyone in the house.

Fixture shutoffs help too

Toilets and sinks have small valves called angle stops. Closing one stops a single leaking fixture without shutting off the whole house. Test them once a year, since they also stick.

Rentals and multi-unit buildings

If you rent or live in a multi-unit building, the main may be controlled by property management. Ask where it is and who to call at night, and keep the number in your phone. Photograph the unit shutoff if there is one.

Guidelines for emergency response, including the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, start with stopping the source, which is why the main valve comes first.

What if the valve leaks or will not hold?

A valve that drips at the handle or will not close completely needs replacing, and a plumber should do it. A leaking main valve does not become safer with time. Until it is replaced, know where the water company's curb valve is and who to call.

Some houses have a pressure reducing valve near the main. It is not a shutoff, and you should not turn its screws. Ask a plumber if you are unsure which valve does what.

Winter checks

If a house will be empty in winter, closing the main and draining the lines reduces freeze risk. Ask a plumber about the water heater and any boiler before you do it. Leaving heat on at a low setting also protects pipes in exterior walls.

Main valves in Glenside

Aqua supplies the water in both Abington and Cheltenham townships, so Aqua is the utility to ask about the meter and the curb valve. PECO supplies electricity and gas, so call PECO for any smell of gas or any sparking.

Glenside homes vary in age, and older valves may be stiff. Our pages for Oreland and Wyncote share local utility notes.
