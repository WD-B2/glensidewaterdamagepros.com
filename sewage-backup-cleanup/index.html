Sewage backup: what to do first

Water rising from a floor drain, a toilet or a basement fixture may carry sewage, and it is an emergency. Sewage backup cleanup removes the contaminated water, discards what it touched and cleans the area, and our line answers 24/7. Keep children and pets out, do not use the plumbing and do not wade in without boots and gloves.

If the water is near outlets, the electrical panel or an appliance, stay out and call PECO or 911 for any electrical risk before you call us. Do not run the washer or flush toilets, since more water will push more sewage into the house.

Why sewage water is different

Sewage is category 3 water, which means it carries contamination from the start. Carpet, padding, drywall and insulation that touched it are usually discarded, since they cannot be made clean again. The EPA recommends using professionals for sewage or contaminated water.

Hard surfaces and framing can often be cleaned and sanitized. Crews wear protective gear, remove wet materials in sealed bags, clean surfaces and then dry the structure. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

Do not clean raw sewage with household methods

Mopping a sewage spill spreads contamination to surfaces and tools. Do not run fans or a household vacuum through it. Leave extraction to professionals and ask your doctor about any health concerns, since we do not give medical advice.

Raw sewage from a basement drain

A basement drain is the lowest opening in the plumbing, so it is where a backup appears first. Water may come from a blocked house line, a problem in the township sewer or an overloaded system after heavy rain. Sorting out which one it is matters for who fixes it.

For the house line, a plumber clears or repairs it. If the problem is in the public sewer, contact the sewer authority. We remove the contaminated water, clean and dry, and our report documents the loss for your insurance claim.

Sewer service around Glenside

Glenside spans two townships with different sewer arrangements. Abington's own wastewater plant serves the western third of the township, and areas east of Edge Hill Road flow to the Philadelphia Water Department's Pennypack interceptor. Ask your township which system serves your street.
