A wet basement is an emergency because water below grade keeps coming and soaks finishes, stored items and the framing. Basement water damage cleanup pumps the water out, removes what cannot be saved and dries the space, and our line answers 24/7. Stay out of deep water, call PECO or 911 for any electrical risk and do not run the furnace while the floor is wet.

When a wet basement is an emergency

Water over the floor drain

The drain is not keeping up, or sewage may be involved.

Sump pump not running

A failed pump lets water rise quickly after rain.

Damp walls with white crusting

Seepage through the wall brings minerals to the surface.

Standing water by the furnace or heater

Power and fuel equipment need a safe approach.

Where basement water comes from

The common causes are a failed or overwhelmed sump pump, seepage through walls and floors after heavy rain, a supply line or water heater failure and a backed-up drain. The cause decides who fixes it. We handle the water and the damage, while a plumber, a waterproofing contractor or the sewer authority deals with the source.

Abington Township's stormwater plan names the Keswick neighborhood as having experienced flooding, and our flood damage page covers larger events. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your township about local drainage.

Finished basements and what is in them

Carpet, pad and drywall in a finished basement are the first things to fail, and they hide moisture in the wall cavity. Crews extract water, cut drywall about a foot above the waterline if needed and dry the framing. Stored boxes and furniture are moved and photographed, and some may need disposal.

Unfinished basements dry faster, because concrete has little to damage. Even so, the walls and the base of the framing should be checked with moisture meters, and for carpeted rooms see carpet restoration.

Restoration is not waterproofing

Drying and repair fix the damage. They do not stop water from entering next time. If the basement floods again after rain, a waterproofing or drainage contractor needs to look at grading, gutters, the sump and the foundation.

We say this plainly so you can plan both jobs, and we are glad to work alongside the contractor who does the waterproofing.

Take stock of what was stored on the floor, and photograph each box before you move it. Paper, cardboard and fabric absorb water and spoil fast, while plastic and metal items can usually be cleaned. A list helps your claim and tells the crew what to move first.

Basement drying is guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, which is also the document an insurer may refer to when it reviews the drying log.

If your house is in Laverock, which sits in both Cheltenham and Springfield townships, our Laverock page explains why sump pump and backflow rules can differ from one side of the line to the other.
