Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak in Glenside, PA: Save It or Toss It?

Furniture is the hardest thing to decide about after a leak. Here is a simple way to sort what to dry and what to discard.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

Soft furniture soaks up water and holds it. A mattress, sofa or upholstered chair can stay wet inside for days even when the surface feels dry. The decision to keep or discard depends on the water type, how long the piece stayed wet and what it is made of.

This guide gives a practical way to sort items after a leak. We do not give medical advice, and if anyone in the home has health concerns about contaminated materials, ask a physician.

Start with the water type

The CDC says to discard cloth, rugs and drywall that touched floodwater, and the EPA recommends using professionals for sewage or contaminated water. If the source is clean and the item was wet only briefly, drying may work.

Time matters

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Past that window, porous items like mattresses and upholstery are more likely to grow mold inside, where it cannot be cleaned.

Mattresses

A mattress has foam and fibers that hold water deep inside. If a mattress was soaked by clean water and dried quickly in a warm, ventilated room, some people save it. If it was soaked for more than a day or touched contaminated water, replacement is the usual answer.

Sofas, chairs and cushions

Frames made of solid wood can often be dried, while particleboard frames swell and fail. Cushions can sometimes be cleaned and dried separately. Leather and fabric with foam inside need the same drying window.

  1. Move the item off the wet floor onto blocks or foil-wrapped supports.
  2. Remove cushions and open the piece to air.
  3. Run a dehumidifier and fans in the room.
  4. Check back in a day for a damp smell or dark spots.

Photograph before you discard

The Pennsylvania Insurance Department advises photographing or recording the damage before cleanup. Photograph each item, note its age and keep a short list with an estimate of what it cost. Keep the item until the adjuster has seen it, unless it is unsafe.

Dry the room too

Furniture is only part of the loss. If the floor and walls stay wet, the pieces you saved can get wet again. See our carpet water damage page for the floor, and structural drying for the rest of the room.

Steps for a sofa that can be dried

  1. Confirm the water was clean, and that the piece has been wet less than about a day.
  2. Pull the cushions and stand them on edge in a dry room.
  3. Raise the frame on blocks and point a fan at the underside.
  4. Run a dehumidifier, and keep checking for odor or dark spots.
  5. Give up on the piece if it still smells damp after a few days.

Items that rarely survive

  • Foam mattresses and pillow-top mattresses
  • Particleboard furniture that has swelled
  • Upholstery touched by sewage or floodwater
  • Any padded item that has stayed wet for days

If you decide to dispose of a large item, check your township's rules first. Bulk pickup and disposal rules differ between townships.

The CDC guidelines on discarding items touched by floodwater and the EPA 24 to 48 hour drying guideline are the references for what to keep, and the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes how contents are handled.

What about rugs, books and clothing?

Washable fabrics can usually be laundered if the water was clean. Wool and synthetic rugs may be cleaned and dried flat, and a rug that sat in contaminated water is usually discarded. Books and paper swell and mold quickly, so photograph and discard them if they stay wet, or freeze them if they matter to you.

Electronics that got wet should stay off. Unplug them, and have a technician look before use. A wet charger or power strip is not safe to reuse.

Dealing with odor on items you keep

A piece that smells after drying is still holding moisture or contamination. Keep it in a dry, ventilated room and check again after a day. If the smell is persistent, the item may need replacement.
