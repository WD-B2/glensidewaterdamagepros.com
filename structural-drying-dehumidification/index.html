The walls feel damp days after the leak

If a wall still feels cool or smells musty days after the water was mopped up, moisture is still inside the structure. That is the job of structural drying and dehumidification, also called water mitigation, which removes the water absorbed into framing, subfloors and drywall. Mopping does not do that, which is why a damp wall can grow mold weeks later.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Starting drying early is the single most effective thing you can do after the standing water is gone.

What the drying equipment does, in plain words

Air movers are high-velocity fans that push dry air across a wet surface so water evaporates faster. LGR dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air and drain it away, so the room does not saturate. Together they dry the surface and the air, and the cycle repeats until the material is dry.

Moisture meters measure the water content of wood and drywall, and a thermal camera shows cool, wet areas behind surfaces. Crews drill small holes or lift baseboards to force air into wall cavities. We do not offer equipment rental, since drying needs readings and judgment, not just machines.

Moisture mapping and the drying log

Moisture mapping is the first step. A crew reads walls, floors and ceilings in a grid, notes the numbers and compares them to a dry reference reading. The numbers set where equipment goes and how long it runs.

Each day the crew rechecks the same spots. Equipment is moved or removed as materials reach dry targets, and the log becomes the proof that drying was finished. The documentation also supports an insurance claim.

How long drying takes

Drywall and carpet often dry in a few days. Plaster, hardwood and concrete can take a week or longer, because they release water slowly. Cold weather, high humidity and the volume of water all change the time, so no honest quote promises a fixed number of days.

Water categories matter too, and clean supply water dries differently from floodwater. If you want to understand the first step, read our page on water extraction, and for floor-specific drying see hardwood floor repair.

A drying plan is written down before equipment is placed. It lists the rooms, the equipment count and the target readings, so you can see why the machines are where they are. Ask for a copy, and ask when the first check-in will happen.

What not to do during drying

  • Do not turn off or unplug the equipment.
  • Do not close wet rooms off with sealed plastic that traps moisture.
  • Do not paint or reinstall trim before readings are dry.
  • Do not walk through areas with cords across wet floors.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the published standard for structural drying, including how moisture is monitored and what counts as dry.

Drying in Glenside homes

Summer humidity in Montgomery County slows drying, so dehumidifiers do more of the work than fans. Houses with plaster walls, as many older ones have, need extra days.
