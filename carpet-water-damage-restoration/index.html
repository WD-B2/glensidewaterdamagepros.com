Wet carpet: save it or replace it?

Carpet and the pad beneath it soak up water quickly, and the pad stays wet long after the surface feels dry. Carpet water damage restoration extracts water, dries the carpet in place where it can be saved and replaces it where it cannot. The first question is the water category, since clean water and contaminated water lead to different answers.

Clean water from a supply line on carpet that is extracted within a day or two can often be saved. Carpet touched by sewage or floodwater is generally discarded, a point the CDC makes for floodwater. If the carpet has been wet longer than 48 hours, the EPA notes that porous materials like carpet may need to be discarded.

How carpet is dried in place

Crews extract with a weighted wand that presses water out of the fibers. They lift the carpet from the tack strip at a corner, place air movers under it and remove the pad if it cannot be saved. A dehumidifier then dries the room, and moisture meters check the subfloor.

Pad is cheap to replace and slow to dry, so it is usually discarded. The carpet itself can be cleaned and reinstalled when the water was clean. For a wet subfloor under the carpet, see structural drying and dehumidification.

Stretching, cleaning and reinstalling

When carpet is saved, it is lifted at one corner, dried over a clean pad or open air and then power-stretched back onto the tack strips. The stretch matters, since wet backing can shrink and leave ripples that never lie flat. A new pad goes underneath, because the old one is the part most likely to hold moisture and odor.

Cleaning after drying removes the minerals and residue the water left behind. If the water was clean, a hot-water extraction clean is typical. If it was not, the carpet is a candidate for replacement.

Tack strips, baseboards and the subfloor

Tack strips along the walls are wood, and they absorb water like any other wood. Crews check them with a meter and replace any that stay damp. Baseboards are often pulled to dry the wall base and reinstalled after.

The subfloor under the pad is the part people forget. Plywood and plank subfloors need to read dry before new pad goes down, because covering a wet subfloor is how odor and mold start. Our crews keep the room open and running until the readings say it is ready.

Odor after wet carpet

A musty smell that lingers after the carpet feels dry usually means the pad or subfloor is still damp, or that the water was not clean. Odor is a reading you can use. If it returns after cleaning, ask the crew to re-check the pad and the subfloor with a meter.

What to do before we arrive

  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks so stains do not transfer.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Keep children and pets off carpet that may hold contaminated water.
  • Open windows only if outdoor air is drier than indoor air.

According to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, the water category drives whether carpet is cleaned in place or replaced, which is why we ask about the source first.
