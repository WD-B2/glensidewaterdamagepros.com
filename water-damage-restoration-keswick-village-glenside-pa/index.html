Keswick Village is the part of Glenside that grew up around Keswick Avenue and the Keswick Theatre. The Abington Township plan says Weldon and Keswick generally refer to the area about a half mile north of the Glenside train station. Our dispatch line serves it with the same 19038 ZIP code as the rest of Glenside.

Most calls here involve ordinary building failures such as a split supply line, an aging water heater or a roof leak. Some also involve water that enters from outside, and the public records on that are worth knowing before you call.

What the public flood studies say about Keswick

Abington Township's stormwater management planning work names Keswick as one of its priority flood-prone study areas. The study report says the flood-prone areas of the Keswick neighborhoods and the Huntingdon Valley Shopping Center have experienced excessive flooding. It tested a 10-year, 24-hour storm of 4.9 inches and compared fixes such as a regional stormwater basin with green infrastructure.

That plan is a study with cost estimates, not a promise that work is finished. The report puts the preferred option at about $31.7 million for construction and says full implementation could take 15 years. It does not list individual properties, so we do not tell any homeowner that a particular house is or is not at risk.

The Tookany Creek flood control project

Part of Glenside lies in Cheltenham Township, where the Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection has been designing a flood protection project along Tookany Creek. Its 2020 presentation lists Keswick Avenue, Brookdale Avenue, Glenside Avenue and Stanley Avenue among the places the work touches. It also records that the levee at the Brookdale Avenue pump station was overtopped in September 1996, June 2001 and September 2011.

The same presentation lists construction dates that were projected in 2020, and we cannot confirm their current status. If your street is near the creek, ask Cheltenham Township about the project and check the effective flood map before you decide anything about coverage.

What older village blocks mean for a wet building

Keswick Avenue carried a trolley line that opened in 1905, and Glenside Gardens, also called Weldon Heights, was developed in 1923. Buildings from these periods can have mixed repairs, so a ceiling stain on a second floor may come from an old roof detail and not from a new leak. We follow the water with meters and thermal imaging and do not guess from age alone.

How extraction and drying work here

Crews remove standing water first, with pumps or wet vacuums, and then measure what is still wet. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days while we log readings in walls and floors. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes this process, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

If water came from a drain or from outside, it is treated as contaminated, and porous materials such as carpet pad are usually removed. Household fans and ordinary vacuums are not enough for that kind of loss. See our water extraction page and structural drying page for the full steps.

Utilities and permits on the Keswick blocks

Aqua supplies water in Abington and Cheltenham, and PECO supplies electric and gas. Sewer service depends on the township, and Aqua's purchase of the Cheltenham sewer system was approved in 2019. Permit rules vary, so ask the building department of your own township before a repair.

Insurance documentation for Keswick losses

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot decide coverage. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help describe the loss, and they do not guarantee a payout.
