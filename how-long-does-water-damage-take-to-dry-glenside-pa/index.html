How Long Does Water Damage Take to Dry in a Glenside, PA Home?

No honest quote gives one number. Here is what changes drying time and how you will know it is done.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

The question most homeowners ask is how many days the fans will run. The honest answer is a range, and it depends on the materials, the amount of water and the weather. This guide explains what controls drying time so you can read an estimate.

We do not promise a fixed number of days. Drying ends when the moisture readings reach dry targets, and that is the only reliable finish line.

The usual ranges

Typical drying times by material
MaterialTypical rangeWhy
DrywallAbout three to five daysThin and porous, dries quickly with air movement
Carpet and padOften two to four days if savedPad is usually replaced
Hardwood floorOften a week or moreDense wood releases water slowly
Plaster wallsA week or moreThick and holds water
Concrete slabOften a week or moreHolds water for a long time

These are general ranges, not guarantees. Conditions on the day can shorten or lengthen them.

What changes the time

  • Amount of water and how long materials stayed wet
  • The type of material, with dense materials slower
  • Indoor humidity, since humid air holds more moisture
  • The number and placement of air movers and dehumidifiers
  • Whether wet materials that cannot dry were removed

Summer humidity in Montgomery County makes dehumidifiers do more of the work than fans. Winter cold slows evaporation, so heat can help.

What the crew is doing while it dries

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces. LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air and drain it away. Moisture meters check wood and drywall each day, and a thermal camera shows cool, wet areas behind surfaces.

Crews move or remove equipment as areas reach dry targets. The daily log shows the trend, and it supports an insurance claim.

Signs drying is not finished

  • A musty smell in the room
  • Walls that feel cool to the touch
  • Flooring that still cups or squeaks
  • A meter reading above the reference dry reading

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. If drying stalls, ask the crew for the log and the plan.

Why equipment noise and heat are part of it

Drying rooms are warm, loud and dry, and that is normal. Keep doors closed to the dry zone and do not turn off the equipment. See our structural drying page for how it works, and hardwood floor repair for floors.

A sample timeline, day by day

What a typical drying job can look like
DayWhat happensWhat you notice
1Water is extracted, moisture is mapped, equipment goes inLoud fans, warm rooms
2 to 3Equipment runs, wet material is removedHumidity falls, smells fade
4 to 5Readings are checked, equipment is movedDrywall nears dry readings
6 and laterDense materials keep dryingPlaster, hardwood and concrete lag

This is a pattern, not a promise. A large loss or dense materials extend it, and a small clean-water loss may finish sooner.

Why a drying job gets extended

  • More water was in the wall than the first readings showed
  • The weather is humid and the dehumidifiers cannot keep up
  • A leak is still active somewhere
  • Dense material such as hardwood needs specialty drying

How you can help

  • Keep doors to the drying area closed
  • Do not turn equipment off or unplug it
  • Report new stains or smells to the crew
  • Keep a note of the equipment count and the reading dates

What to do while the equipment runs

  • Keep cords off wet floors and away from walkways.
  • Do not cover or unplug the dehumidifiers.
  • Keep children and pets away from the fans.
  • Call the crew if a new stain or smell appears.

What a drying log looks like

A drying log lists each room, the reading in each spot and the date it was taken. The numbers should fall day by day. If they stall, the crew changes the equipment layout or finds a source of moisture they missed.

Ask to see the log at the end of the job, and keep a copy. It shows your insurer and any future buyer that the structure was dried to a standard, and it is more reliable than anyone's promise of how many days it would take.

Drying in Glenside homes

Glenside homes vary in age, and plaster walls take longer than drywall. The 2020 Census counted 7,737 residents across the Abington and Cheltenham sides, and houses on both sides range widely in construction.

If you are in Wyncote, where the historic district was listed in 1986, or in Flourtown, older plaster construction is more common, and drying plans allow for it.
