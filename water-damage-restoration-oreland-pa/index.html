When water is spreading in Oreland

Oreland is a census-designated place split between Springfield and Upper Dublin townships, with 6,210 residents in the 2020 Census and the ZIP code 19075. Which township your house sits in changes who handles sewer, permits and water, so the first question on an Oreland call is the street address. The emergency dispatch line is open around the clock, and same-day response is available when crews are free.

Do not wait for the water to stop on its own. A supply-line leak keeps running until it is closed, and a basement that is taking on rain needs the pump kept working. While you wait for the crew, keep a list of what is wet, because the dispatcher will ask.

Two townships, two sets of rules

The Springfield side of Oreland is served by Bucks County Water and Sewer Authority, which took over the township sewer system on January 1, 2016. Springfield Township makes it illegal to connect a sump pump to the sanitary sewer, requires floor drains to be disconnected and requires backflow prevention on below-grade fixtures, installed by a registered plumber with a permit. If your basement drains into the sanitary line, that is a code problem to fix before the next storm.

The Upper Dublin side is served by several water companies, and the township says its service map is only a rough guide. Check your water bill to see whether it comes from Aqua Pennsylvania, Ambler Borough Water Company or North Wales Water Authority. Knowing the provider tells you whom to call if you cannot find or close the main.

Oreland landmarks and what they tell you about the housing

Limekiln Pike is the long road through Oreland, and the Emlen House on its edge served as George Washington's headquarters in 1777. Houses near these older roads are more likely to be of older construction with original supply lines and cast iron drains. The Lansdale/Doylestown SEPTA line runs through town and brings commuter housing close to the tracks.

Older supply lines fail at joints and fittings, and a burst pipe is a typical Oreland call. Drainage after storms is the other source, so the basement cleanup page is relevant here.

When to call 911 or PECO first

  • Call 911 for a gas smell or a risk of electrocution.
  • Call PECO for sparking, a buzzing panel or a downed line.
  • Call the 24/7 restoration line once the area is safe.
  • Tell the dispatcher which township your house is in.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration applies in both townships that share Oreland, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.
