What Is Water Mitigation? A Guide for Glenside, PA Homeowners

Mitigation is the part of a water loss that stops the damage from growing. Here is what it covers and what it leaves to later stages.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

Water mitigation is the set of steps that stops water damage from spreading and keeps a wet building from getting worse. It comes first in a water loss, ahead of repairs, and it is the stage where speed matters most. In Glenside, PA, most homeowners hear the word from an insurer or a contractor and are not sure what it covers.

This guide explains mitigation in plain words, how it differs from restoration and remediation and what the first 48 hours look like. It also says what mitigation does not do, because that is where most confusion starts.

Mitigation, restoration and remediation are three different jobs

Three terms, three stages
TermWhat it meansTypical work
MitigationStop and limit the damageStop the source, extract water, dry the structure
RestorationReturn the property to pre-loss conditionReplace drywall, flooring, paint and trim
RemediationRemove a specific contaminantMold removal or sewage cleanup under a standard

Mitigation is the emergency stage, restoration is the rebuild and remediation is a specialized cleanup, such as mold. Many companies do all three, and an insurance file may list them as separate line items. Knowing which stage you are in helps you read an estimate.

What mitigation includes

  1. Stop the source. Close a valve, shut off the main or have a plumber or roofer stop the entry of water.
  2. Extract standing water with pumps, wet vacuums and extraction wands.
  3. Map the moisture with meters and a thermal camera, so wet areas that look dry are found.
  4. Remove materials that cannot be dried, such as soaked pad or crumbling drywall, to prevent mold.
  5. Dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers, and log readings daily until materials reach dry targets.

The industry standard that describes this work is ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration. A homeowner cannot read the standard line by line, but you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Why the first 48 hours matter

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and says the key to mold control is moisture control. That window is the reason mitigation is urgent. Waiting a week to start drying a wall that was wet on day one means the repair will be larger.

If you are waiting for a crew, you can still help. Shut off the water, move small items to dry surfaces and photograph the damage before cleanup. The Pennsylvania Insurance Department recommends photographing or recording the damage before cleanup and saving receipts.

What mitigation does not do

Mitigation does not waterproof the house. If water enters the basement after every storm, mitigation dries the damage, and a waterproofing or drainage contractor deals with how it gets in. A roof that leaks needs a roofer, and a failing pipe needs a plumber.

Mitigation also does not promise that the finished house will match the old one. Restoration handles paint, flooring and trim. Ask for the mitigation and rebuild scopes in writing, so you can compare them with the insurance estimate.

How to tell if the mitigation was done well

  • A drying log with dated moisture readings for the same spots
  • Equipment placed in the wet rooms, not just one room
  • A clear explanation of what was removed and why
  • Scope and price explained before work started

If a crew cannot show readings, you do not know the structure is dry. Insurers also ask for the log, since it supports the claim and shows the job reached a dry standard.

Mitigation in three real situations

A washing machine hose splits on the second floor. Mitigation closes the valve, extracts water from the laundry room, checks the ceiling below with a meter and dries both rooms before anything is replaced. The goal is to stop water from getting into the wall and ceiling cavity.

A roof leak soaks attic insulation over a weekend. A roofer closes the opening, and the mitigation crew removes the wet insulation and dries the ceiling from above and below. Without that step, the stain returns after every rain.

A sump pump fails in a finished basement. The crew pumps out the water, removes the wet carpet and pad and dries the walls and slab. Waterproofing, if needed, is a separate project.

What to do before the mitigation crew arrives

  • Stop the source of water.
  • Move small items to dry surfaces.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup.
  • Keep power off to wet areas if you can do it safely.
