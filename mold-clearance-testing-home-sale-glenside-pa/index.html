Mold Clearance Testing for a Home Sale or Loan in Glenside, PA

A buyer or lender may ask for proof the mold work is finished. A clearance report is how that proof is usually shown.

By the Glenside Water Damage Pros team

24/7 dispatch line
(445) 263-2078
Call (445) 263-2078

Clearance testing is a check, done after mold remediation, that a treated area has returned to normal conditions. It is common when a home is being sold, refinanced or insured and someone wants documented proof. It is not a guarantee that mold will never return, because mold returns when moisture does.

This article explains how clearance works in practice, who performs it, what the report usually contains and how to prepare if you are selling in Glenside, PA. It is not legal advice, so ask your real estate agent or attorney what disclosure rules apply to your sale.

What clearance means and does not mean

A clearance check usually includes a visual inspection, a moisture reading and sometimes air or surface samples compared with a reference. A pass means the treated area looks and tests normal at that moment. It does not say what will happen after future water events.

Why an independent tester is common

The person who tests is usually not the person who removed the mold. That separation lets a buyer or lender trust the result, since the tester has no stake in the work. If a lender or buyer names a form or a tester, ask before work starts.

Ask for the report in writing, including where samples were taken and what the lab compared them with. If a pass depends on a condition, such as keeping the area dry, ask for it to be written down.

Preparing for a sale

  • Document the source of the moisture and that it was fixed
  • Keep the remediation scope, photos and moisture logs
  • Ask your agent or attorney what has to be disclosed
  • Keep the clearance report with your sale documents

Buyers often ask about past leaks and mold. A complete record answers the question in a page. It also helps if a buyer's inspector finds a stain, since you can show it was found, fixed and checked.

What a clearance report cannot do

It cannot prove the house will never have mold again. It cannot say anything about health, and health questions belong with a physician. It also does not replace a seller's duty to disclose, which your agent or attorney can explain.

Where remediation fits

Remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. If you are earlier in the process, our mold remediation page explains inspection, containment and removal. If the mold followed a leak, see ceiling repair or structural drying for how the moisture was handled.

A sample timeline for a sale

  1. Find and fix the moisture source, and get an invoice.
  2. Remediation crew removes the affected material and dries the area.
  3. Moisture readings confirm the area is dry.
  4. An independent tester performs the clearance check.
  5. Documents go into the sale file, and the buyer's agent is told they exist.

Build in time for each step, because lab results often take days. If you list a house while work is underway, tell your agent so they can plan around it.

Questions to ask a tester

  • Are you independent of the company that did the work?
  • How many samples, and where will you take them?
  • What reference will the results be compared with?
  • What will the report look like and how fast will I get it?

Ask the same of any tester a buyer or lender suggests. A report you cannot read is not worth the paper it is printed on.

When to call before you disturb the area

  • Do not scrub or disturb a large area before the tester has seen it.
  • Do not paint over the area before the clearance check.
  • Keep the moisture logs and receipts where you can find them.
  • Ask your agent or attorney about disclosure before you list.

What a buyer's inspector may ask

An inspector may ask when the mold was found, what caused it and what was done. They may also ask for the clearance report and the moisture log. Having them in a folder shortens the conversation and can keep a sale on track.

If the inspector finds new growth after your clearance, the moisture source may have returned. Do not argue, check the source and call the crew back. Mold is a sign of moisture, and moisture is the problem to fix.

Selling a Glenside home

Glenside straddles Abington and Cheltenham townships, and Abington Code Enforcement handles building permits on its side. If repairs needed a permit, keep the permit record with your sale papers, and ask the township building department what applies.

Buyers in the area may also check the FEMA flood map for the property address, so be ready to share what you know. If you are comparing nearby towns, our pages for Wyncote and Flourtown explain local drainage and sewer notes.
