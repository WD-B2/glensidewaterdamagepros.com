Cupped, crowned or buckled boards after a leak

Hardwood takes up water through its underside and edges, so the damage often appears days after a leak. Hardwood floor water damage repair dries the boards and subfloor and decides whether sanding and refinishing will do or whether boards must be replaced. Cupping is the edges rising above the center, crowning is the center rising, and buckling is boards lifting off the subfloor.

Do not sand or refinish while the wood is still wet. Sanding a cupped floor before it is dry can leave a dished surface when the boards flatten later, and finish applied over moisture traps it.

Edges higher than the center

Classic cupping, from moisture below the boards.

Center higher than the edges

Crowning, often after cupped boards were sanded too soon.

Boards lifting from the subfloor

Buckling means the wood expanded so far it released.

Dark staining or a musty odor

Staining and odor suggest water has sat under the boards.

How hardwood is dried

Crews use moisture meters to read each board and the subfloor. Specialty drying mats pull water up through the wood with vacuum and heated air, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the room. The process can take a week or more, and readings tell us when to stop.

Some boards recover their shape and others do not. We check flatness again once the wood is dry, and only then decide about sanding. A kitchen floor wet by a dishwasher is a common case, which our appliance leak page covers.

Sanding and refinishing, in order

Refinishing follows drying and a flatness check. Once the boards read dry and have settled, a floor sander removes the cupped edges and the old finish, and new stain and coats go on. Sanding too soon removes more wood than needed and can leave a dished floor when the boards flatten.

A floor can only be sanded a limited number of times, so the thickness of the wear layer matters. Engineered flooring has a thin top layer and may not survive a deep sanding, while solid hardwood usually has more to give.

Gaps after drying

Boards shrink back as they dry, and gaps can appear between them. Small gaps may close with the next humid season, and large ones can be filled or boards replaced. Ask the crew what to expect before the final finish goes on.

What not to do to a wet hardwood floor

  • Do not sand or seal the floor while the boards are wet.
  • Do not cover the floor with plastic or rugs that trap moisture.
  • Do not use a heater to force-dry the boards, since fast drying can crack them.
  • Do not walk on buckled boards, which can shift and snap.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration covers the specialty drying that dense materials like hardwood need, and it is the benchmark you can ask a floor contractor or drying crew to follow.
