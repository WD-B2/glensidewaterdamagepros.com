Ardsley is a village in Abington Township, and it uses the Glenside ZIP code 19038. The Abington Township plan says it was planned as North Glenside in 1918 and 1919 and developed by W.T.B. Roberts. Our crews reach it from Glenside, and this page covers what is different about calls from Ardsley.

Ardsley is not part of the Glenside census-designated place, so it appears here as a nearby community that we serve. It is a separate village with its own station, park and school history.

What the township plan records about Ardsley

The plan says the Ardsley train station was built in 1897 under the name Tyson, and that the post office opened in 1922. Ardsley Elementary opened in 1932, and the parks map shows Ardsley Park, the Ardsley Community Center and the Ardsley Wildlife Sanctuary. Hillside Cemetery, which the plan says now covers 180 acres, connects to the wildlife refuge.

Those details matter to a restoration call for one reason. Large green areas and the village layout mean that houses sit on a mix of streets, and the right answer to a wet basement depends on the street and the source. We do not publish housing-age or basement figures for Ardsley, because the plan does not give them.

Which sewer line serves your street

Abington Township says its own treatment plant serves the western third of the township, and that wastewater east of Edge Hill Road flows into the Philadelphia Water Department Pennypack interceptor. Which route your sewer takes depends on where your street sits, so ask the Abington wastewater department before you assume. A backup is easier to sort out when you know whether the blockage is in your house line or in the public main.

If a toilet or floor drain is bubbling up, stop using water and keep people away from the area. Sewage water is contaminated, so our sewage backup cleanup page explains why household fans and wet vacuums are not enough.

How drying is done

Water is extracted first, and then air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure while readings are logged each day. The crew decides which wet materials can be saved and which must come out, because not every material can be saved. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides that decision, and you can ask any company to explain how it applies it.

Mold needs moisture to grow, so drying matters, but nobody can promise that mold will never develop. If you find growth, see our mold remediation page, and ask your physician about any health concern.
