Warm spots and a running meter: is it a slab leak?

A slab leak is a break in a pipe buried in or under the concrete floor. People notice it as a warm spot on the floor, a water meter that keeps moving with every tap closed or damp flooring with no obvious source. Slab leak water damage cleanup starts with a plumber finding the break, and our line is open 24/7 when water reaches the surface.

If water is already coming up through flooring, shut off the main and call. Slab leaks only apply to a house or an addition built on a concrete slab, so this page is for those cases. We do not publish local frequency figures, and we would rather describe the mechanism than guess at how often it happens.

How the damage spreads

Water under a slab rises through cracks and joints and soaks flooring and baseboards from below. Hardwood cups, laminate swells and carpet pads hold the water for weeks. The moisture can also wick up into drywall, which is why damp lower walls are a clue.

Drying a slab takes longer than drying a wood floor. Concrete holds water and releases it slowly, so crews use moisture meters and dehumidifiers and test again before closing up. Floor covering that sits on a wet slab, such as hardwood or vinyl plank, is often removed so the slab can dry.

When the plumber finds the break

The plumber chooses between breaking the slab to repair the pipe and rerouting the line through the walls or attic. Rerouting avoids tearing up the floor but changes where the pipes run. Ask your plumber and your insurer how each choice is covered, and keep the invoice with the moisture log.

Drying a slab and the flooring above it follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and readings from the slab itself are the proof that it is dry.
